Sound familiar?

“We've been doing this work for twenty years. We're good at it. So why does the tender always go to someone else?”

If you've ever asked that question — usually late at night, halfway through a quality response — you're in the right place. And the answer usually has nothing to do with how good you are at the job.

The part nobody tells you

Tenders aren't won by the best business. They're won by the best answer.

Evaluators never visit your sites. They never meet your team. They score what's on the page — against a marking scheme, under time pressure, alongside a pile of other bids. That changes everything about how a response should be written.

1

Evaluators score, they don't read.

They're working through a marking scheme with a deadline of their own. If the points aren't easy to find, they aren't awarded — no matter how good the work behind them is.

2

Knowing the job isn't the same as evidencing it.

You carry twenty years of know-how in your head. The panel can only score what makes it onto the page, in the shape the question asked for.

3

10pm is no time to write your best work.

Most SME bids are written after hours, around the day job. Tired writing reads as tired thinking — and it scores that way too.

How I help

Four ways to take the weight off.

Every business arrives in a different place. Some have a deadline this Friday. Some are tired of near-misses. Some just want to be ready for the next opportunity before it lands.

01 — When the deadline is real

Bid & tender writing

A tender has landed. It matters. And it's competing with everything else on your desk. I take the questions, sit down with the people who actually do the work, and write the response — in your voice, built around what the marking scheme rewards.

You stay across it without carrying it. Every draft comes back to you with time to review, not at midnight before the deadline.

What changes The submission goes in on time, reads like your best day, and you get your evenings back.

02 — When you keep coming second

Bid reviews & scoring

You're already writing bids — and losing by a mark or two. Before your next submission goes in, I score it the way a panel would: question by question, against the criteria, with no politeness.

You'll see exactly where points are leaking — the assumed knowledge, the buried evidence, the answers that describe the work instead of answering the question.

What changes You stop guessing why you lost. The next bid goes in knowing where every mark comes from.

03 — Before the next one lands

Tender readiness

The worst time to gather policies, case studies and evidence is the week a tender arrives. I build your bid library in the quiet weeks — accreditations, social value, case studies written up properly, answers to the questions that appear in every tender.

It's the difference between starting from a blank page and starting from eighty per cent done.

What changes The next opportunity is a decision, not a scramble. You bid because it's right for the business — not because there's just enough time.

04 — When bidding becomes regular

Ongoing bid support

Growing businesses reach a point where tenders arrive monthly, not yearly — but not the point where a full-time bid team makes sense. I become that team: watching frameworks, qualifying opportunities honestly, and writing when it's worth writing.

Including telling you when a tender isn't winnable. Your time matters more than my invoice.

What changes Bidding becomes a steady, deliberate part of how the business grows — not an emergency that hijacks a fortnight.

How it works

Simple on purpose.

You're busy. The process respects that. Most clients spend a few hours across the whole thing — the rest is mine to carry.

1

We talk.

A straightforward conversation about the tender, the business, and whether it's genuinely worth bidding. If it isn't, I'll say so — that advice is free.

2

I dig in.

Short calls with the people who know the work — the contracts manager, the supervisor, whoever holds the detail. I turn what's in their heads into evidence a panel can score.

3

You review, I refine.

Drafts arrive early enough to actually read. You mark up what's not quite right; I fix it. Nothing goes out the door until it sounds like you on your best day.

4

Submitted — with room to breathe.

Complete, compliant, uploaded ahead of the deadline. And after the result, a proper debrief either way — because every tender should make the next one easier.
