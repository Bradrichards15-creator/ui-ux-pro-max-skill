**[Hero markup card - sits beside the headline]**
**[Label]** Response as submitted

**[Body text, five phrases underlined in red, footnoted below]**
If successful, we intend to mobilise the contract over 12-14 weeks(1). We will use a job tracker(2) to plan and manage onboarding, and are willing to appoint a named contract manager(3) who will follow the KPIs(4) set out in the specification. We will also deliver community benefits(5) throughout the contract term.

**[Footnote 1 - heading]** 12-14 weeks
**[Footnote 1 - body]** The requirement says 10 weeks. You're already telling the panel you'll take longer than they've asked.

**[Footnote 2 - heading]** "a job tracker"
**[Footnote 2 - body]** Which one? Who manages it? What does the buyer actually get to see?

**[Footnote 3 - heading]** "willing to appoint"
**[Footnote 3 - body]** That's not a commitment. If the contract requires one, say who it will be and what they'll be responsible for.

**[Footnote 4 - heading]** "follow the KPIs"
**[Footnote 4 - body]** What does that mean in practice? Measure them? Report them? Review them? The panel needs to know.

**[Footnote 5 - heading]** "deliver community benefits"
**[Footnote 5 - body]** Which benefits? How many? By when? Who benefits? And how will you demonstrate that you've delivered them?

**[Closing heading]** The problem
**[Closing text]** None of these statements give the evaluator much to score. They sound like commitments, but most of the important detail is left for the panel to assume.
